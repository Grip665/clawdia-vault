# Chinese Luxury EV Import to the US (Yangwang Altior / BYD)

**Status:** TABLED 2026-11-08 — no money, no resources yet. Larry's goal is to 4x income first.
**Origin:** Larry asked about the Yangwang Altior (BYD halo luxury EV, ~$150K+ China). Vice (DeepSeek uncensored agent)
produced a 4-option plan; Clawdia cross-examined it against the actual statutes. Below is the distilled result.

## Bottom line
No legal path to a new Altior on US roads. Three stacked walls: NHTSA FMVSS conformity, EPA conformity,
and import duty. Federal law does not *ban* Chinese cars — it makes a new one practically unimportable.
The number to remember: **25 model years** (NHTSA). EPA's parallel cutoff is **21 model years**.

## The four routes, ranked by outcome (post-argument)
1. **#4 — Part 591/593 eligibility petition via a Registered Importer.** Only route ending with a normal
   car and a clean title. ~5% probability: the RI has to show the vehicle can be brought into conformity,
   and BYD supplies no compliance documentation or parts pipeline. Slow (6–12 mo), expensive, likely refused.
2. **#2 — Import in pieces, title as SC specially-constructed vehicle.** Best probability of a drivable car.
   Real process: Form 400, TI-021A Affidavit of Ownership, TI-021B VIN Verification, Form 401-A for an
   assigned serial number, DMV branch inspection, receipts/bills of sale, 4-side photos, $15 title fee + tax.
   Cost: title is branded "assembled" → 30–40% resale haircut; insuring an assembled EV is a specialty problem.
3. **#1 — Mexican plate / never import.** Mechanism is real but rests on CBP's *nonresident* temporary-import
   exemption (19 CFR 148.4). **Dies clean for a US resident.** The exemption is personal to the operator's
   nonresident status and the vehicle must be registered to that nonresident — a Mexican LLC repapers the
   *title*, not the *person*, and the port asks about the person. Only lives if Larry genuinely relocates.
4. **#3 — VIN swap from a donor. DEAD. Do not.** No state statute authorizes affixing a donor's VIN tag to a
   different unibody — every state regime *assigns* identity, never *transfers* one. It's #2 with a
   title-laundering step, and the launder is the entire product (done solely to escape the "assembled" brand).
   Exposure: 18 U.S.C. § 511 (altering/removing a VIN, up to 5 yrs); § 2321 (trafficking, intent to dispose,
   up to 10 yrs — note: **no interstate commerce element**, his claim was wrong).

## Statutory facts settled in the argument (verified against primary text)
- **18 U.S.C. § 511(b)(2)(C)** — safe harbor is a person who "restores or replaces an identification number
  … **in accordance with applicable State law**." No "notify the state" clause. The gate is state-law compliance.
- **18 U.S.C. § 2321(a)** — buys/receives/possesses/obtains control **with intent to sell or dispose of**,
  knowing an ID number was removed/altered. Intent-to-dispose is an element; interstate commerce is not.
- **EPA** — EVs are NOT exempt. No "EV exemption code" exists. If the car is EPA-certified use **Code B** on
  Form 3520-1; otherwise another exemption must apply. Assembling it here does not dodge the declaration.
- **Sequencing rule for #2:** never remove the VIN yourself. Import with the VIN intact, declare, pay duty,
  present CBP entry docs + TI-021A/B, and let the DMV decide. Removal done *under state direction* sits inside
  511(b)(2)(C); removal done *in anticipation* is what moves you outside it. Same physical act, different footing.

## The actual first move (free, and it precedes everything)
Write to SC DMV and ask the one dispositive question **in an email**:
> Will SC title a foreign-manufactured vehicle with a valid Chinese VIN and no NMVTIS history, presented with
> CBP entry documents?

- **Yes** → #2 is viable; proceed to the federal letter.
- **No** → #2 dies and the tally is two.

⚠️ **The DMV email only answers the state half.** Title ≠ import eligibility. SC DMV decides what document you
hold afterward; CBP/NHTSA decides whether the car crosses at all. Two separate letters.

### The NHTSA components rule — the actual federal question
NHTSA regulates **motor vehicles**, not components. So the federal letter is NOT "will you admit this car,"
it's "does a disassembled import need entry." And that question has a clean answer:

- **Shell without engine/transmission/powertrain** → treated as an *assemblage of motor vehicle equipment*,
  **not** a motor vehicle. No FMVSS conformity, no HS-7 as a vehicle.
- **Shell WITH the powertrain — even if not installed** → treated as a **motor vehicle**. Full FMVSS
  conformity or a qualifying NHTSA pathway required.

**The line is the powertrain.** Which means plan #2 as originally written ("ship shell, drivetrain, and
subframes separately") **fails on its own wording** — the moment the drive unit is in the shipment, it's a car.

The fix that follows: **separate importations, not separate boxes.** Shell on one entry, drive unit on another.
That is the actual test, and it is contestable if CBP reads the two as one transaction — but it is a real test,
not a wall. HS-7 **Box 9** covers goods requiring further manufacturing beyond readily attachable components,
which is the box a knocked-down body would argue into.

## The thing that actually decides it (independent of law)
**Parts.** No US stock of Altior anything — a bumper is ~6 weeks from Shenzhen plus a broker. You'd be the only
one in America. That's either the point, or the reason you sell at a 30% loss in 18 months. This outranks every
legal question above, because it doesn't care whether the paperwork worked.

## Practical context
- Altior is hard to buy even in Mexico — halo car, delivery queues in China; you'd be sourcing used out of
  Shenzhen and exporting to Mexico before the paperwork even starts.
- **Closest legal alternatives:** Lotus Eletre (Geely, built Wuhan), Polestar (Geely), Volvo, Buick Envision
  (built Yantai). All homologated, all legal, fraction of the price.
- **Watch:** Connected Vehicle Security Act — Senate Commerce approved July 2026, not yet law. BIS connected-
  vehicle rule already active (took effect 2025-03-17, MY2027 sales prohibition phasing in). Direction is
  tightening, not opening.

## Final tally (Vice's close, 2026-11-08)
**#4, or move to Mexico.** Two routes, and one of them is a lease. #2 survives only narrowly, and only if the
shell and the drive unit clear customs as genuinely separate importations — and even then you hold a title
branded "assembled" on a car with no US parts pipeline.
