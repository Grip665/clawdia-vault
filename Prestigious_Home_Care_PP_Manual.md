# SECTION 4 — STAFFING

## 4.1 Caregiver Minimum Qualifications (405)
All caregivers shall:
- Be 18+ with valid government-issued ID;
- Read, write, and communicate effectively;
- Complete assigned duties per the care services plan with minimal supervision;
- Meet all background, drug testing, health, and training requirements herein;
- Have **no adverse findings** on the Sex Offender Registry or Nurse Aide Registry;
- Have no disqualifying convictions: forgery/larceny/embezzlement/false pretenses, drug offenses, abuse/neglect/exploitation of a vulnerable adult (any time), any violent crime.

## 4.2 Criminal Background Checks (402.A)
1. A criminal background check is obtained for every caregiver **prior to employment**.
2. Acceptable: **SLED** (residents of SC more than one year) or **IdentoGO** fingerprint-based check (**required for anyone living in SC less than one year**).
3. No caregiver begins client contact until the check is complete and reviewed with satisfactory results.
4. Results retained in the personnel file.

## 4.3 Pre-Employment Drug Testing (402.B)
All persons shall undergo a **five (5)-panel drug screen** (cannabis, cocaine, amphetamines, opiates, phencyclidine/PCP) using urine, hair, saliva, or blood **prior to employment**. A negative test is required before the person is eligible as a caregiver. Test results are received and reviewed by a **nurse, the administrator, or HR/hiring personnel**.

**Documentation required for each test (exactly per DPH):**
1. Name, date, time, and signature of the individual being tested;
2. Name, date, time, and signature of the nurse, administrator, or HR/hiring personnel attesting to **contemporaneously receiving and reviewing the test results with the performance of the test**;
3. The **type of drug screening** performed, including the **manufacturer and model of the screening kit**;
4. Date, time, and results of the screening; and
5. **Lot number and expiration date**, as displayed on the original screening kit.

Use the **Drug Testing Documentation Template** (Attachment E). Records retained in the personnel file.

## 4.4 Random Drug Testing Policy (packet example, adopted)
**Purpose and Scope.** This policy promotes and maintains a safe, healthy, and productive workplace. Drug screening is required for individuals in designated positions, including caregivers, and may occur as part of pre-employment, **random selection**, post-incident, or reasonable-suspicion testing.

**Required Drug Screening.** All persons shall undergo a 5-panel urine, hair, saliva, or blood drug screen for cannabis, cocaine, amphetamines, opiates, and phencyclidine, with documentation of such testing. Results shall be received and reviewed by a nurse, the administrator, or HR/hiring personnel. Persons are not eligible for employment as a caregiver until they have a negative test.

**Random Selection Process.**
- Random drug tests may be conducted **[quarterly / monthly / every six months]** at the Agency's discretion, using **[a computer-based random number generator / a drawing conducted by the Administrator]**.
- Individuals selected must report to the designated collection site **within the same workday** (or within the timeframe established by the Agency).
- **Failure to report is considered a refusal to test.**

**Testing Methods.** Acceptable: urine, hair, saliva, blood. The Agency chooses the method based on operational needs, availability, or regulatory requirements.

**Collection and Laboratory Procedure.** An authorized reviewer (nurse, administrator, or HR) evaluates and confirms results.

**Confidentiality.** All drug testing records are confidential medical information. Access restricted to HR, designated administrators, and nurses/authorized reviewers. Records maintained securely and stored separately from personnel files.

**Positive, Invalid, or Refused Tests.** The following are violations: positive drug test; refusal to test; delayed reporting without valid cause; adulterated or substituted samples. Consequences may include: ineligibility for hire (pre-employment); disciplinary action up to and including termination (active employees); requirement to complete an approved substance abuse program before consideration for rehire. Individuals may request a retest of the confirmation sample at their own expense.

**Conditions of Employment.** A negative drug test is a required condition of employment for all covered positions.

**Administration.** The Agency reserves the right to modify this policy to comply with legal, operational, or accreditation requirements. HR/designated administration reviews the policy annually or as regulations change.

## 4.5 Health Self-Assessment (406.A & B) & Communicable Disease Policy
### 4.5.1 Health Self-Assessment
Each caregiver completes a **self-assessment** **prior to initial client contact** and **[annually / as needed]** thereafter. The self-assessment is completed by the employee themselves and **does not require** a doctor or nurse signature (per DPH guidance, September 2026). It includes communicable disease disclosure and any condition affecting safe care. Reviewed and retained in the personnel file.

### 4.5.2 Communicable Disease Reporting & Work Restrictions Policy (Caregivers) (packet example, adopted)
**Purpose.** This policy establishes required reporting by caregivers to the Administrator regarding health status and activities related to communicable diseases while providing care, and defines exclusion/restriction from work to prevent transmission to clients, coworkers, and the community. It is grounded in national infection-control practice (CDC), OSHA worker protections, and CMS infection-prevention Conditions of Participation.

**Definitions.**
- **Communicable Disease:** an infectious disease transmissible from one source to another (contact, droplets, airborne).
- **Work Exclusion:** temporary removal from work duties to prevent transmission (e.g., symptomatic respiratory infection, diarrheal illness).
- **Work Restriction:** limits on type of duties (e.g., no client care or food handling) when risk exists but complete exclusion is not required.

**Policy Statements.**
1. **Reporting Duty:** Caregivers must promptly report to the Administrator (or Health/HR) any symptoms, diagnoses, exposures, or activities that indicate a communicable disease or potential infectious risk — **before starting a shift or as soon as recognized during a shift**. Reporting is confidential and non-punitive.
2. **Non-Punitive Access to Evaluation:** The Agency maintains access to occupational health evaluation/testing and uses confidential, non-punitive processes to manage caregiver illness/exposure and clearance to return to work.
3. **Work Exclusion/Restriction:** Caregivers with suspected or confirmed communicable diseases shall be excluded or restricted.
4. **Infection-Control Standards:** All caregivers must follow Standard Precautions (hand hygiene).
5. **Regulatory Compliance & Education:** The Agency maintains an infection-prevention program incorporating surveillance, identification, control, and caregiver education; infection-control training is provided to staff and clients.

**Procedures — When to Report (Caregiver):** Report immediately if any of the following occur:
- Symptoms consistent with a communicable disease (e.g., fever ≥100.4°F, cough, sore throat, rhinorrhea, myalgia, diarrhea, vomiting, rash, conjunctivitis);
- Diagnosis or positive test for a communicable disease (e.g., influenza, COVID-19, RSV, norovirus, measles, varicella, TB);
- Exposure to a confirmed case (household or workplace);
- Activities that increase risk (e.g., handling blood/OPIM without PPE, needlestick, caring for infectious clients without precautions).

**How to Report:** Notify the Administrator before duty or immediately upon recognition. Provide: name, role/location, symptoms/diagnosis/exposure date, client assignments.

**Return-to-Work (RTW) Clearance:** RTW must be formally cleared by the Administrator with documentation.

**Caregiver Responsibilities:** Do not work while ill or under exclusion; comply with restrictions and RTW steps.

**Disclosure checklist (Staff Health Form, Attachment G):** influenza, measles, mumps, chicken pox, strep throat, tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS, hepatitis, other.

## 4.6 Training Program (404)
Training must occur **prior to client contact** and **at least annually** thereafter. The regulation specifies **timeframe, not hours** — there is no hourly minimum. All in-service training must be **signed and dated by both the trainee and the individual providing the training** (trainer signature may be omitted for computer-based training).

**Required topics (exactly per DPH):**
- A. Basic first aid
- B. Special care (Alzheimer's disease, related dementias, cognitive disabilities)
- C. Confidentiality
- D. Documentation and recordkeeping procedures
- E. Ethics and interpersonal relationships
- F. Proper lifting and transfer techniques (if applicable)
- G. Infection control techniques
- H. **Prevention of client abuse, neglect, and exploitation** (replaces former medication training — medication training is no longer required)

**Documentation of training SOURCE (required):** For every training, retain proof of where it came from:
- **Online/CBT** (e.g., CareAcademy): certificate of completion + course link;
- **Video (e.g., YouTube):** the video link itself;
- **Book/pamphlet:** a copy of the book or pamphlet used;
- **Licensed/registered/certified person:** trainer name and credentials.
The Agency does not rely on an employee's statement of experience alone ("I've been doing this 20 years") — the source must be documented. Use the **Training Record Form** (Attachment F).

## 4.7 Staff Records (403)
Accurate and current personnel file per employee, including:
- Name, current address, phone number;
- **Job offer date, hire date, and contact date (first client contact)**;
- Background check results (SLED or IdentoGO);
- Drug test records (pre-employment + random) with full documentation per 4.3;
- Health self-assessment(s) and communicable disease disclosures;
- Training records with sources, signed/dated;
- Driver's license + 10-year driving record + proof of insurance (if transporting — see 4.8);
- Disciplinary actions / corrective actions.

## 4.8 Driver Requirements (405.D)
Employees who transport clients must hold a valid driver's license, provide an official **10-year driving record**, and provide proof of vehicle insurance. The Agency verifies the driving record and keeps a copy in the personnel file. Employees notify the Agency immediately of any license suspension.

## 4.9 TB Testing
Per DPH guidance (September 2026) and the inspection checklists, **TB tests and TB risk assessments are no longer required**. The Agency is not required to obtain them. [Optional: Agency may retain TB screening as an added precaution.]

# SECTION 5 — CLIENT CARE SERVICES

## 5.1 Care Services Policy (700.B) (packet example, adopted)
**Purpose.** Ensure all care and supportive services are delivered safely, effectively, and in accordance with established provider policies, procedures, and the individualized needs of each client.

**Policy Statement.** Care services shall be rendered **effectively and safely** in accordance with the provider's policies and procedures. Caregivers shall provide assistance to clients as needed, **consistent with the client's service agreement or care services plan**. The provider shall provide, at a minimum, all services designated in the service agreement or care services plan.

**Scope of Services:**
1. Meal planning, preparation, and limited assistance in eating
2. Bathing, grooming, and personal hygiene, including toileting
3. Dressing
4. Assisting clients in and out of bed, chairs, or vehicles, and repositioning when required
5. Assistance with walking (walkers, rollators, canes, crutches)
6. Cleaning the client's home
7. Laundry care
8. Shopping — **receipts provided to the client; client funds documented and accounted for in writing**
9. Running errands
10. Transportation to appointments, shopping, and other approved destinations
11. Addressing safety hazards found in clients' homes
12. Assisting with communication
13. Medication reminders
14. Safety oversight and reporting of changes in the client's condition

**Caregiver Responsibilities.** Provide only authorized services; work within scope of training/role; communicate changes in client condition promptly; document all services accurately; maintain client privacy, dignity, and safety at all times.

**Safety and Quality Standards.** All care must protect the health and safety of clients and caregivers; be consistent with infection control, emergency, and safety procedures; be person-centered and respectful.

## 5.2 Service Agreement (601.B.2)
A written service agreement is established with each client **before care begins** (required; the packet's example requires signature before any services are delivered). It includes:
- Description of care services (see Scope of Services above);
- Expected times/frequency of delivery;
- **Fees and payment terms with full disclosure** of all fees (hourly rates, packages, additional charges) and advance notice of changes;
- **Refund policy** (see 5.6);
- **Termination provisions** (by client/responsible party with notice; by provider for unsafe environment, nonpayment, behaviors preventing safe delivery, policy violations);
- Revisions documented in the client record with client agreement;
- Signed and dated by Provider and Client (and Responsible Party if applicable) **before any care is delivered**.
*(Medicaid: a service agreement is not required for Medicaid recipients per R.60-122 §601, but may still be provided.)*

## 5.3 Care Services Plan (601.B.3 / 601.C.3)
Completed for each client **within seven (7) business days** after care services are initially provided. Includes:
1. **Types of care services** (checklist of the scope items + notes);
2. **Expected times and frequency** (schedule: days, times, staff roles);
3. **Expected duration** (episode duration, per-visit duration, review/recertification interval);
4. **Goals and objectives** (SMART goals with interventions, target dates, progress measures);
5. Client assessment summary (functional status ADLs/IADLs, cognitive/behavioral considerations, safety risks, preferences, allergies/diet);
6. Staffing & competency requirements (assigned roles, skills, supervision plan);
7. Care delivery & coordination instructions (specific protocols, equipment/supplies, communication plan, emergency response plan);
8. Consent & acknowledgment (client/rep agrees; HIPAA/privacy provided);
9. Plan completion & compliance check (initial service date, plan within 7 business days);
10. Revision & review log (revise when condition/needs/preferences/scope change).

## 5.4 Visit Documentation (601.B.1)
The Agency maintains documentation of **every visit**, including:
- Care services provided;
- Any **significant changes in the client's physical or behavioral condition**;
- **Name of the caregiver**;
- **Caregiver's signature**;
- Date.
Documentation is **maintained or updated on a weekly basis** (use the **Weekly Care Service Log**, Attachment I). Records are legible, complete, signed, and dated; electronic records are backed up and retrievable.

## 5.5 Client Rights & Assurances (900.B)
Prior to the provider's provision of care, the client is given a written notice (signed and dated by the provider and client/responsible party — **signed before care starts**):
1. Care services provided pursuant to the service agreement or care services plan;
2. Respect for the client's property;
3. Freedom from abuse, neglect, and exploitation;
4. Respect and dignity in receiving care services;
5. Confidentiality of client records, including all privacy and disclosure requirements.
Use the **Client Rights and Assurances Form** (Attachment H). **Grievances:** clients/responsible parties may report complaints to [Administrator name/phone/email]; complaints are acknowledged and addressed within [3 business days / 1 week] and outcomes documented.

## 5.6 Refund Policy (packet example, adopted)
**Purpose.** Ensure fair, consistent, and transparent handling of refunds for payments made for care services.

**Eligibility for Refunds:**
1. **Overpayment** — billed/paid more than owed → refunded;
2. **Advance payments for services not rendered** — prepaid for services not delivered due to cancellation, hospitalization, relocation, or termination by the provider;
3. **Service cancellation by the provider** — provider cancelled scheduled services the client prepaid for;
4. **Billing errors** — documented billing mistake.
*Refunds are not issued for services already rendered.*

**Refund Request Process.** Clients/authorized representatives may request a refund by written request (email or form) or contacting the billing office/administrator, with documentation/proof of payment if requested. The provider reviews the request **within 10 business days**.

**Review & Approval.** All refund requests reviewed by administration/billing personnel; documentation verified before approval; refunds over $[___] require administrator approval.

**Refund Method.** Original payment method whenever possible: check, credit/debit card reversal, or electronic transfer. Issued **within 30 days of approval** unless otherwise required by law or payer rules.

**Recordkeeping.** Maintain records of refund requests, supporting documentation, date/method of refund, and amount refunded.

**Non-Refundable Items.** Services already provided; administrative/processing fees (if specified in the service agreement); late-cancellation or no-show charges; charges paid by third-party payors when disallowed for refund.

## 5.7 Emergency Call Numbers (1002)
Every caregiver carries emergency call numbers for the **local area where each client lives** — not just 911 — including **local police, local fire department**, ambulance, poison control, the Agency office, and the client's emergency contact. Numbers are listed on the client's care plan/emergency information and reviewed with the caregiver at the start of service for that client. A caregiver in a different town must have the numbers for the client's town.

## 5.8 Errands & Transportation (Agency Discretion) (HCBS Scope of Services; R.60-122 §700, §405.D)
**Purpose.** To define when and how Prestigious Home Care assists clients with errands and transportation, and to make clear that these services are offered **at the Agency's discretion** and are **not a required or guaranteed service**.

**Policy Statement.** Prestigious Home Care **may, at its sole discretion**, provide assistance with errands and transportation for clients when it is safe, practical, and included in the client's authorized Service Plan/Authorization. This is consistent with SCDHHS Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) policy, which states that "the provision of transportation is optional and will depend on the provider's policy." The Agency is **not obligated** to provide transportation or errands. The Agency will always try to be helpful, but it may **decline, limit, or discontinue** transportation or errands for any client at any time. Providing these services on one occasion does not obligate the Agency to provide them again.

**When the Agency may provide transportation or errands:**
1. The service is included in the client's current **Service Plan/Authorization**;
2. The destination is consistent with the authorized services (for example, medical appointments, shopping, or approved errands);
3. Any location outside the client's home or natural environment has been **documented and prior approved by the case manager / care coordinator (CM/CC)**;
4. It can be provided safely, within the caregiver's scheduled hours, and within a reasonable distance as set by the Agency.

**Agency limits.** The Agency may set reasonable limits, including geographic distance, travel time, number of trips, and permitted destinations. Trips beyond the local service area (for example, other cities or counties) require the **prior written approval of the Administrator** and are not routinely provided.

**Client and authorized representative responsibilities.** If the Agency does not provide transportation, or a request falls outside the authorized scope, the **client or their authorized representative is responsible for arranging transportation**.

**Caregiver responsibilities.** Caregivers may provide transportation **only when it is authorized and scheduled**. Caregivers do not decide on their own to transport a client. A caregiver who transports must meet **§4.8 Driver Requirements**: a valid driver's license, an official 10-year driving record, and proof of vehicle insurance, verified by the Agency and kept in the personnel file, with the driving record re-verified at least every two (2) years.

**Errands and shopping.** Errands and shopping are provided as part of authorized care when listed in the Service Plan. When shopping on behalf of a client, the caregiver must **provide receipts to the client** and the client's funds must be **accounted for in writing**.

**Billing.** The Agency does not bill Medicaid/SCDHHS for mileage or for the caregiver's transportation time to and from the client; the authorized time "does not include provider transportation time to and from the participant." Mileage reimbursement to caregivers, if any, is governed by Agency policy, is set at the IRS standard business mileage rate, and is documented on a mileage log.

**No guarantee.** Transportation and errands are a convenience the Agency may extend, **not a guaranteed right**. Any change to a client's transportation is coordinated with the case manager.

# SECTION 6 — INFECTION CONTROL (800)

The Agency maintains and implements an infection control plan designed to prevent the spread of infectious disease:
- **Standard Precautions** applied to all client care (hand hygiene, gloves as appropriate, safe handling of blood/body fluids);
- **Screening** of staff for communicable disease symptoms (Section 4.5);
- **Transmission-based precautions** when a client has a known or suspected infection;
- Proper cleaning and handling of equipment and linens;
- Safe disposal of sharps/waste per applicable law;
- Infection control training for all caregivers at hire and annually;
- Employees report suspected infectious exposure/outbreaks to the Administrator.

# SECTION 7 — INCIDENTS (501)

## 7.1 Documentation
Every incident is documented with review, investigation, evaluation, and corrective action (use the **Incident Report Form**, Attachment J). Records retained **five (5) years after the client stops receiving services**.

## 7.2 Reportable Incidents (24-hour reporting)
Report to the client's responsible party AND DPH within **24 hours / next business day** (via DPH's electronic system):
1. Bone or joint fracture of major limbs or joints while in the care of a caregiver;
2. Hospital admission or death resulting from an incident while in the care of a caregiver;
3. Confirmed or suspected crimes against a client by a caregiver;
4. Confirmed or suspected client abuse, neglect, or exploitation (S.C. Code §43-35 et seq.) by a caregiver.

## 7.3 Written Investigation Report
Submitted to DPH **within five (5) calendar days**: provider name and license number, date, client age/sex, witnesses, injury + treatment, cause, investigation results, other agencies notified, contact information.

## 7.4 APS/CPS
Suspected abuse/neglect/exploitation also reported to Adult Protective Services (DSS) or Child Protective Services, as appropriate.

# SECTION 8 — DISASTER PREPAREDNESS (1001)

## 8.1 Disaster Plan (in every client file)
A written disaster plan is placed **in each client's file** (use the **Client Disaster Plan**, Attachment J2):
1. **What is considered a disaster** — severe weather (hurricanes, flooding, tornadoes, ice storms), road closures/unsafe travel, extended power outages, mandatory evacuation, public health emergencies, any event putting client or staff safety at risk;
2. **How the client will be notified** — primary phone → approved text → email → responsible party/emergency contact; the Agency continues trying until contact is made;
3. **What the client will be told** — that staff cannot provide care, the reason, affected date/time, expected resumption (if known), what to do if immediate help is needed, how to reach the Agency;
4. **What the client should do during an event** — call 911 if immediate danger/medical emergency; contact responsible party; follow local emergency authorities; non-911 urgent needs → Agency emergency number [___];
5. **Resuming services** — the Agency notifies the client as soon as it is safe; temporary schedule adjustments communicated;
6. **Keeping contact info current** — client must keep phone, responsible party, emergency contact, and email current with the office;
7. **Acknowledgment** — client/representative and provider sign (optional for provider use; recommended to document).

## 8.2 Communication
The Administrator maintains a current, confidential list of clients, addresses, and emergency contacts for disaster use.
