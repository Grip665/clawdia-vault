06.10 Introduction

   Service planning encompasses a comprehensive review of the participant's strengths,
   resources, needs, and conditions. The Case Manager will assist the participant in setting
   goals based on the participant’s desires. The service planning process is determined by
   the participant and/or Authorized Representative in conjunction with the SCDHHS
   worker and/or Medicare – Medicaid Plan (PRIME) designee and allows for
   participation of the physician, service providers and other individuals identified by the
   participant and/or Authorized Representative. Service planning provides the participant
   with information necessary to make informed choices regarding the location of care
   and services to be utilized.

   Service planning includes service coordination with other agencies such as home health,
   case management, or other involved entities, etc., to ensure all services (waiver and
   non-waiver) are considered in the development of the service plan. (Refer to Section
   05.34)

   Completion and implementation of the service plan is a function of the Case Manager
   and all involved parties.

 06.20   Service Plan Policy and Procedure

    06.21 Development of the Initial Service Plan
         Service Plans must be person-centered. Prior to the development of the Service Plan, the
         participant and/or Authorized Representative must be contacted by the Case Manager to
         facilitate a discussion regarding the participant’s strengths, preferences, goals and needs.
         Initial service planning begins during the waiver enrollment transfer conference held with
         the Nurse Consultant. The Service Plan is developed by the enrolling Case Manager in
         conjunction with the Nurse Consultant. The enrolling Case Manager and Nurse
         Consultant must discuss the assessment information and include information gathered from
         the team conference, participant, and/or Authorized Representative’s input, and agencies
         providing services to the participant. Once the assessment information is discussed, it is the
         enrolling Case Manager’s responsibility to document the discussion in a narrative. This
         information will assist the enrolling Case Manager in identifying the participant’s
         preferences, needs and goals.

         Several factors influence the service planning process and the determination of the services
         to be authorized. These factors include:

         1.     The participant’s expressed need for assistance and the value assigned to that need.

         2.     The amount and type of assistance in place.

         3.     The degree of assistance, and the time required to complete the needed care.

         4.     The participant’s cognitive ability, strengths, and preferences.

         5.     The availability of other cost-effective resources to meet these needs.

         6.     The participant’s enrollment in Hospice. (Please refer to Hospice Benefit Interactions
                located in Phoenix HELP.)

         7.     The participant is residing in a Community Residential Care Facility. (Please refer to
                Waiver Interactions with Community Residential Care Facility located in Phoenix
                HELP.)

         Active participation and planning with the participant and/or the Authorized Representative
         regarding the participant's long-term care is an integral part of the CLTC Program. Development
         of a realistic and thorough Service Plan and its implementation in the community involves
         numerous contacts and extensive planning and coordination.

         Service planning must address the participant’s desires, strengths and needs identified
         through the assessment process as well as viable solutions. It must include resources
         currently utilized by the participant, both waiver and other, as well as additional services
         which may be available to meet the participant's needs. All payment sources, where

    06.22 Purpose of the Service Plan
        The results of the service planning process are documented on the Service Plan. It is the
        intent of the Service Plan to be a multidisciplinary plan rather than a nursing care plan. A
        completed Service Plan is required for all waiver participants.

        Each Service Plan should be individualized for a participant and completed so that a service
        professional unfamiliar with the participant can have, by reading the plan, a clear picture of
        the participant’s needs, strengths, preferences, planned interventions, and person(s)
        performing the interventions.

    06.23 Elements of the Service Plan
        The Service Plan includes seven (7) elements:

        •       Personal Goals

        •       Problems/Needs

        •       Goals

        •       Strengths

        •       Waiver/ Non-Waiver/Caregiver Supports

        •       Planned Services and Interventions

        •       Service Plan Participant Agreement

        The service plan is expected to address all assessment components identified as an active
        need or goal. The service plan Comments Sections should be used only for insertion of
        clarifiers and should not be used as a Narrative.

        The initial Service Plan must be completed within seven (7) business days of the
        participant’s waiver enrollment.

        EXCEPTION: Since the person-centered planning process requires the participant
                  and/or Authorized Representative to select the time and location and
                  whom they wish to be involved in the Service Plan development, the
                  Case Manager may not always be able to meet the seven (7) business

            06.23.10     Personal Goals
                Personal Goals are determined by a discussion with the participant or Authorized
                Representative, if participant is unable. The Case Manager will ask if the participant
                has any Personal Goals and what they are. Personal Goals should be realistic and the
                steps to achievement may involve waiver supports or other means.

                The Case Manager will list the steps identified by the participant and/or Authorized
                Representative to achieve this goal. The Case Manager must indicate when the steps
                are achieved or terminated.

                At a minimum, the participant’s Personal Goals must be addressed at the initial visit,
                quarterly visit, and re-evaluation visit. It may also be appropriate to address these
                during monthly contacts. The Personal Goals must be clearly documented in the
                Service Plan.

                Personal Goals are an important part of person-centered planning and reflect what is
                important to the participant. All goals should be SMART:

                •       Specific

                •       Measurable

                •       Achievable

                •       Relevant

                •       Time-limited

                Personal goals must be:

                    1. Stated in positive terms, not in terms of what should be avoided;

                    2. Defined in terms of the expected outcome (a result or condition to be
                       achieved rather than an activity to be performed);

                    3. Written in quantifiable (measurable) terms, so that all involved persons may
                       know when the goal is reached;

                    4. Achievable, taking into consideration known resources;

                    5. Designed as a joint commitment between the participant and the Case

            06.23.11    Needs
                In order to develop a plan for intervention, a need must be clearly defined. The
                Service Plan must address all areas in which the participant requires at least
                limited/moderate assistance. Each assessed need with corresponding goals will be
                populated on the Service Plan. All identified needs/goals should be those which the
                Case Manager, participant, and Authorized Representative will be actively working
                to meet.

                When the Case Manager identifies services that are needed but are unavailable,
                these services should be noted in the body of the Service Plan as a need and identified
                as an unmet need under the intervention. If an identified service need is not populated
                on the Service Plan, custom needs, goals, and intervention must be added.

                Each listed need must have corresponding goals and interventions.

            06.23.12    Strengths

                Identifying a participant’s strengths is an important part of the assessment/service
                planning process. When thinking about content to enter, consider strengths that help
                the participant remain in the best possible health while living in their chosen setting.

                Examples of strength areas include but are not limited to:

                •       Social relationships/connections

                •       Family supports

                •       Mental health

                •       Self-care skills

                •       Talents/interests

                •       Education/training

            06.23.13    Caregiver Supports and Non-Waiver Supports

                The Caregiver Supports and Non-Waiver Supports sections of Phoenix contain
                information regarding the family and other non- waiver caregivers assisting the
                participant in the community. All corresponding fields in Phoenix should be
                completed.

                        caregiver, an interview questionnaire (Caregiver Interview) MUST be

                        However, this questionnaire can be updated any time during the service plan
                        year if the caregiver exhibits an increase in stress.

                        A Caregiver Supports reference guide is available under Phoenix Help

            06.23.14     Waiver Supports
                The Waiver Support Section in Phoenix is used to house information regarding waiver
                services in place for the participant. It is also used to generate Service Provision Forms
                (Authorizations) for waiver services.

              06.23.15   Planned Interventions

                 Once a need/goal has been established, interventions should be selected to reach the
                 goal.

                 In general, interventions are actions taken to improve a situation. The appropriate
                 interventions must be chosen to address each need/goal within each section of the
                 Service Plan.

              06.23.16   Service Plan Participant Agreement

                 The Service Plan Agreement Section is part of the current Service Plan and is
                 presented to the participant and/or Authorized Representative as the last page of the
                 service plan. The Service Plan Agreement Section serves as a record that the Service
                 Plan has been developed, reviewed, and evaluated with the participant and/or
                 Authorized Representative. The Service Plan Agreement Section is signed and dated
                 at the Initial Visit AND during the participant's first quarterly visit following a re-
                 evaluation visit. The dated Service Plan Agreement Section is signed by both the
                 Case Manager and the participant and/or Authorized Representative. It is used on
                 an on-going basis and is maintained in Phoenix.

                 If a hard copy Service Plan Agreement Form is needed, the signed hard copy form
                 must be scanned into Phoenix Scans under the “Service Plan Agreement” scan tag.
                 A copy of the Service Plan must be given to the participant and/or Authorized
                 Representative at the first in-home visit following the creation of a new Service Plan.
                 If revisions to the Service Plan are needed following the in-home visit, a copy of the
                 revised Service Plan must be mailed to the participant and/or Authorized
                 Representative, as long as an Authorized Representative Form 1282 CLTC is on file
                 to share information with someone other than the participant.

                 Diagnoses are auto populated on the Service Plan from the assessment information
                 entered into Phoenix.

          06.24.11 Psychosocial

                 If the waiver participant indicates that he or she is experiencing social isolation or
                 withdrawal from leisure activities as new or existing behaviors, the Case Manager
                 should discuss these behaviors with the waiver participant and document responses
                 in Phoenix.

                 A Custom Needs/Goals/Interventions must be entered on the Service Plan for
                 socially isolated or withdrawal from leisure activities behaviors.

  06.25       Initial Service Plan Completion and Implementation
          The initial Service Plan must be completed within seven (7) business days of waiver
          enrollment. The Service Plan is complete upon team staffing and signing.

          Once completed, the Service Plan is available to all appropriate waiver service providers in
          Phoenix, except Attendants. The Case Manager will provide a copy of the Service Plan to
          Attendants after the completion of the Attendant Care Training. If training is not required,
          the Case Manager will mail a copy of the Service Plan to the Attendant.


  06.26       Service Plan Revision within 365 Calendar Day Cycle
          The Service Plan may require revisions when changes in the participant's condition, service
          needs and/or preferences occur which do not affect the re-evaluation cycle itself. Any
          additions or revisions in the Service Plan, including waiver, caregiver supports, and other
          supports, within the re-evaluation cycle must be entered into the Service Plan. Upon receipt
          of a request to modify the service plan, the Case Manager will discuss the request with the
          participant and/or Authorized Representative and review/modify the service plan within two
          (2) business days.

          When additions or revisions are made to the Service Plan during the 365-calendar day cycle,
          the affected provider(s) must be notified of the Service Plan changes via phone call or
          Conversation feature in Phoenix. Attendants must be notified by phone or CLTC
          Notification Form.

  06.27     Team Involvement
        Service planning is always a team effort. Staffing for levels of care and service plan reviews
        at enrollment and re-evaluation must be completed with the SCDHHS worker (or the
        Medicare – Medicaid Plan (PRIME) designee for PRIME waiver participants) in a manner
        where both parties can see the most current information in Phoenix. (This may be the Case
        Manager II, Lead Team Case Manager, Area Administrator, Lead Team Nurse
        Consultant, Nurse Consultant or Medicare – Medicaid Plan (PRIME) designee.) Team
        signatures are completed in Phoenix.

  06.28      Service Plan at Re-evaluation
        Ensuring the Service Plan’s effectiveness and accuracy is an on-going process.

        At re-evaluation, the new Service Plan Interventions should reflect only the current services
        received. Service Plans must be person-centered. The participant and/or Authorized
        Representative and any other parties which the participant and/or Authorized Representative
        choose to be included in the service planning process must be contacted for their input in
        evaluating the effectiveness of the Service Plan, and any changes that have occurred in the
        participant’s condition or support system. It may be appropriate to include the physician,
        providers, home health nurse or others involved in the participant’s care.

        Service Plan re-development must be completed within three hundred sixty-five (365)
        calendar days of the last assessment date. Service Plans are to be re-developed at the same
        time as the assessment and have the same effective date. If the assessment is greater than
        thirty (30) days from Service Plan completion date, a new Assessment must be
        completed. The Service Plan must be created, completed, staffed, and signed in Phoenix,
        and all data must be uploaded to Phoenix, by the three hundred and sixty-fifth (365th)
        calendar day of the previous assessment date. The completed assessment for re-evaluation
        visit, Level of Care, and Service Plans must be completed by the due date.

                Example:
                Current Level of Care and Next Level of Care and Service Plan
                Service Plan Date         Completion Due Date
                 August 15, 2020               August 14, 2021


        A participant who experiences a significant change in condition requires an immediate
        review of the Service Plan. Participants who experience frequent changes impacting service
        planning, may require Service Plan review more frequently than every 365 calendar days.
