03.10         Introduction

              Level of Care determination is the process of identifying the extent of a person's
              medical, psycho-behavioral, and functional abilities in keeping with the South
              Carolina Level of Care Criteria for Medicaid-Sponsored Long-Term Care. By
              applying these criteria, an applicant's requirement for skilled or intermediate care can
              be determined.

03.20    Level of Care Policy and Procedure

        03.21    The Level of Care Determination Process

                Following assessment completion, an SCDHHS worker authorized to complete
                level of care team staffing evaluates the applicant's assessment. To clarify the
                assessment information, the SCDHHS worker may consult with the applicant
                and/or Authorized Representative, physician, hospital/nursing home assessor, and
                approved SCDHHS team member with regard to medical, psycho- behavioral, and
                functional information. A complete Phoenix assessment or review of a completed
                1718 and/or review of medical records submitted by the facility are required to
                determine level of care.


                Once the SCDHHS worker determines that adequate information has been
                obtained to make a level of care decision, an SCDHHS worker team conference
                is held to discuss the case. The level of care recommended in Phoenix is considered
                during the team conference. A final level of care is determined from the team
                conference and assigned in accordance with the South Carolina Level of Care
                Criteria for Medicaid-sponsored Long-Term Care. If the level of care determined
                at the team conference is not the level of care recommended by Phoenix, the level
                of care must be reviewed by the Area Administrator or Lead Team Nurse
                Consultant. The third signature must be completed by Central Office. Submit
                Central Office requests to the Waiver Service Exception Group via email at
                WES@scdhhs.gov.


                If the level of care is tentative, the SCDHHS worker annotates the tentative level
                of care in the narrative and sends the tentative level of care to the appropriate
                entity. The SCDHHS worker will not complete the level of care section for a
                tentative level of care. The assessment will be updated, and a final level of care
                should be signed by the SCDHHS worker team upon the final level of care
                determination. If the SCDHHS worker team is unable to determine the level of
                care, the SCDHHS worker annotates this in the narrative, including the reason for
                the inability to reach a decision. If the team was unable to determine level of care
                or if the physician insists that the applicant requires long term care services, the
                team should confer with the Area Administrator and/or the designated Lead
                Team. The Area Administrator may recommend that the case be forwarded to
                Central Office for input/determination of the level of care. (See also 3.22 if the
                level of care appears to be Medically Ineligible.)


                The level of care and the justification for the level of care determination must be
                properly documented by the SCDHHS worker in the Phoenix narrative.

      03.22    Medically Ineligible

              If upon the assessment visit, an applicant appears to be medically ineligible, and
              chooses to proceed with the assessment, the SCDHHS worker will seek medical
              information from the physician by mailing or faxing the Physician’s Input Letter
              and the CLTC Consent Form 121. Three (3) attempts within ten (10) business days
              should be made to contact the physician’s office to obtain medical information for
              the Physician’s Input Letter and the CLTC Consent Form 121. These attempts
              must be clearly documented in Phoenix narratives. No entries should be entered
              on the “source of information/Level of Care” section in Phoenix until information
              is received from the physician. If the physician does not respond in the allotted
              time frame, the assessment may be discussed with another approved nurse with the
              information available.


              Evaluation of the medical information (See Chapter 2, Section 02.60.10) obtained
              from the physician will ensure that the applicant is at the appropriate level of care
              and that the case record is complete for possible appeal proceedings.


              Once an applicant has been determined to be medically ineligible, the applicant is
              ineligible for CLTC services and for Medicaid-sponsored nursing home
              placement. However, determination of medical ineligibility does not mean that the
              applicant cannot be admitted to a long-term care facility under some other payment
              source.

             When an applicant has been determined to be medically ineligible, a Level of Care
             Certification DHHS Form 185 for nursing home application, or a CLTC
             Notification Form 171, for community-based waivered services application, is
             used to formally notify the applicant and/or Authorized Representative of this
             decision. This denial informs all individuals involved in the application process
             that the applicant is ineligible for Medicaid reimbursed skilled or intermediate
             care. This notification must include the appeals notice and the reason for the denial.
             A copy of the Certification DHHS Form 185 or CLTC Notification Form 171 must
             also be sent to Medicaid Eligibility.


             If the applicant appears to be medically ineligible at any time while the case is
             pending, a visit must be made to the applicant to re-determine the level of care.
             The case should be reviewed with the designated Lead Team or Area
             Administrator prior to rendering a final decision. If the Area Administrator
             determines the case requires further review, it should then be referred to the
             Waiver Service Exceptions Group (via mail WES@scdhhs.gov) for a final level
             of care decision. All applicants who have a skilled need or require a skilled
             service in absence of a functional deficit must be referred to the Waiver Service
             Exceptions Group (via email WES@scdhhs.gov) for a level of care
             determination. Once the level of care is determined by the Waiver Service
             Exceptions Group, the Waiver Service Exceptions Group will notify the SCDHHS
             worker of the Level of Care Determination and the required third signature may
             be signed by the Area Administrator, Lead Team or Central Office Designee.

             Special efforts should be made to discuss the level of care decision and possible
             alternatives with the applicant and/or Authorized Representative. The SCDHHS
             worker should inform the applicant to contact CLTC Area Office if there is a
             change in the applicant’s health status during the determination process.

             If the applicant’s final level of care determination is medically ineligible, the team
             signs the final level of care in the Assessment level of care section of Phoenix. The
             SCDHHS worker terminates the program application in Phoenix. A CLTC
             Notification Form 171 or a Level of Care Certification DHHS Form 185 is
             prepared and forwarded. The SCDHHS worker completes correspondence as
             appropriate to applicant, scans any forms and documents to the appropriate scan
             tag and routes to
             the designated support staff for mailing.


             If someone is suspected of being medically ineligible, the assessor should not enter
             data on the Level of Care page in Phoenix until all information is received from
             the physician and other sources and the case is actually discussed/staffed as being

      03.23    Timeliness Standard

              From the point that the assessment information is complete and adequate to
              determine the level of care, the level of care must be determined and documented
              within three (3) business days.


              There may be times when clarification of an applicant’s medical condition or
              additional information is indicated and may interfere with the established time
              frames. Any exceptions of these frames must be documented in the Narrative.


              The initial waiver level of care must be team conferenced by two SCDHHS
              workers. The date of the assessor’s signature is the date the assessment
              information is complete. The date of the reviewer’s signature and level of care date
              is the date of the team conference. (For waiver participants, see also Chapter 5,
              Section 05.38.)

              NOTE: Once a tentative level of care is established, the assessment is to be
              completed and final level of care issued within thirty (30) calendar days. The
              timeliness standard to obtain all necessary documents on a tentative level of care
              is thirty (30) calendar days.


              The application for hospitalized applicants should be terminated if discharge
              is not imminent after thirty (30) calendar days using the closure reason,
              Tentative LOC > 30 days. Phoenix will not automatically close a tentative level
              of care after thirty (30) calendar days.


              If the final level of care assessment is not completed within thirty (30) calendar
              days, Phoenix will automatically archive the assessment but will keep the
              application opened. The SCDHHS worker will close the application as
              appropriate.


              The timeliness standard for Out of State applicants to move in State is sixty (60)
              calendar days. If the applicant has not moved in State within sixty (60) calendar
              days, then use closure reason “Did Not Relocate”. (see Chapter 8, Section
              08.55.10)

      03.24    Level of Care Determination for Hospital-Based Applicants

              For hospital-based applicants seeking nursing home admission or community-
              based services, the level of care determined by the SCDHHS worker team, prior
              to discharge, will be a tentative level of care. A visit or telephone interview with
              the applicant and/or Authorized Representative or other knowledgeable entity is
              required on the initial tentative level of care to secure or verify assessment
              information and obtain written choice for location of care for community-based
              services. When the assessment is completed by telephone interview, supporting
              medical documentation must be obtained from the hospital. This may include
              history and physical, nursing notes, therapy notes, laboratory/medical test results,
              etc. This tentative level of care is especially helpful to hospital discharge
              planners/social workers, applicants, and/or Authorized Representatives as nursing
              home placement is pursued. The tentative level of care must be documented in the
              Narrative. The team's tentative level of care decision must be transmitted by the
              CLTC Notification Form 171 to the hospital discharge planner/social worker and
              applicant and/or Authorized Representative. The SCDHHS worker instructs the
              designated Support Staff to mail the form as indicated.


              Once a tentative level of care has been established, the assessment information
              must be reviewed when nursing home placement is imminent to verify the
              applicant's status and condition.

              The hospital discharge planner must call the SCDHHS worker on the day of
              discharge to review the applicant's condition with a SCDHHS worker. If the
              condition of the applicant has not changed significantly since the tentative level of
              care was determined, the team can proceed with completing the Source of
              Information/Level of Care section in Phoenix without additional assessment or
              discussions. This also applies if the applicant's functional status has not changed
              since the tentative level of care, but skilled services have been decreased or
              discontinued. The tentative team conference Narrative must include the skilled
              service and what the level of care would be if skilled service discontinued. If the
              discharge planner does not inform the SCDHHS worker of the level of care prior
              to discharge, then the assessment will remain incomplete. A new referral would
              be created and the pre-admission screening packet from the nursing home is
              required.

         03.24.10    Medicare Referral

                    Medicare hospital insurance can help pay for inpatient care in a Medicare-
                    certified skilled nursing facility.

                    To qualify for Medicare-sponsored nursing home placement, the applicant
                    must be eligible for Medicare/Part A benefits.


                    •       The beneficiary must be an inpatient of a hospital for a medically
                            necessary stay for at least three (3) consecutive calendar days;
                            (Medicare – Medicaid Plan (PRIME) members do not have to meet this
                            three (3) consecutive calendar day requirement).

                    •       Services must be needed for a condition which was treated during the
                            applicant's qualifying hospital stay, or by a condition which arose while
                            the applicant was in the facility for treatment of a condition previously
                            treated in the hospital;

                    •       Applicant must be admitted to a participating skilled nursing facility
                            within thirty (30) calendar days of discharge from an inpatient
                            hospital stay where they must require and receive a covered level of
                            care;

                    •       Applicant requires skilled nursing services or skilled rehabilitation
                            services;

                    •       Applicant requires a single goal-directed therapy or a combination of
                            goal-directed therapy services five (5) sessions per week;

                    •       Daily skilled services can be provided only on an inpatient basis in a
                            skilled nursing facility; and,

         03.24.20       Hospice Interaction with Nursing Facility Certification

                    If an applicant receives a Medicare qualifying skilled service for a
                    condition unrelated to the terminal diagnosis, Medicare will pay the
                    nursing facility and the hospice benefit.
                    If an applicant receives a Medicare qualifying skilled service for a
                    condition related to the terminal diagnosis, Medicare will only pay the
                    hospice benefit. In this situation, CLTC will certify for Medicaid if
                    Medicaid criteria is met.


                    If the applicant is not Medicare eligible, certify as usual.

                                         Quick Reference for Level of Care Checklist

Skilled Services (SKS)
SKS 1. Daily monitoring/observation and assessment due to an unstable medical condition which may include overall management
          and evaluation of a care plan which changes daily or several times a week.
SKS 2. Administration of medications which require frequent dosage adjustment, regulation, and monitoring.
SKS 3. Administration of parenteral medications and fluids which require frequent dosage adjustment, regulation, and monitoring.
          (Routine injection(s) scheduled daily or less frequently [such as insulin injection] do not qualify.)
SKS 4. Special catheter care (e.g., frequent irrigation, irrigation with special medications, frequent catheterizations for specific
          problems.)
SKS 5. Treatment of extensive decubitus ulcers or other widespread skin disorder. (Important considerations include: Signs of
          infections, full thickness tissue loss, or requirement of sterile technique)
SKS 6. A single goal-directed rehabilitative service (speech, physical, or occupational therapy) by a therapist 5 sessions per week.
          Combinations of therapies will satisfy this requirement.
SKS 7. Time-limited, goal-directed, educational services provided by professional or technical personnel to teach self-maintenance,
          such as education for newly diagnosed or acute episodic conditions (e.g., medications, treatments, procedures).
SKS 8. Nasogastric tube or gastrostomy feedings.
SKS 9. Nasopharyngeal or tracheostomy aspirations or sterile tracheostomy care.
SKS 10. Administration of medical gases (e.g., oxygen) for the initial phase of condition requiring such treatment, monitoring
          and evaluation (generally no longer than two-week duration).
SKS 11. Daily skilled monitoring or observation for conditions that do not ordinarily require skilled care, but because of the
          combination of conditions, may result in special medical complications. In these situations, the complications and the skilled
          services required must be documented.
SKS 12. This individual is totally dependent in all activities of daily living: incapable of locomotion; unable to transfer; totally
          incontinent of urinary or bowel function; must be totally bathed and dressed and toileted and need extensive assistance to
          eat.

A person must need at least one of the numbered skilled services (Items 1-11, adapted from the Medicare requirements at 42 C.F.R.
409.32-35 [1993]) and have at least one of the numbered functional deficits listed below to qualify for skilled level of care. A person
needing item #12 by itself qualifies for skilled level of care because this represents a total care individual. In order to qualify as a
skilled service, the service must be ordered by a physician, require the skills of professional or technical personnel, and be furnished
directly by or under the supervision of such personnel [42 C.F.R. 409.31-35 (1993).] The need for skilled services must be clearly
documented in the client's record.

Intermediate Services (IS)
IS 1.    Daily monitoring of a significant medical condition requiring overall care planning in order to maintain optimum health
         status. The individual should manifest a documented need, which warrants such monitoring.
IS 2.    Supervision of moderate/severe memory, either long or short term, manifested by disorientation, bewilderment, and
         forgetfulness which requires significant intervention in overall care planning.
IS 3.    Supervision of moderately impaired cognitive skills manifested by decisions which may reasonably be expected to affect an
         individual's own safety.
IS 4.    Supervision of moderate problem behavior manifested by verbal abusiveness, physical abusiveness, or socially
         inappropriate/disruptive behavior.

Functional Deficits (F)
F1.      Requires extensive assistance (hands-on) with dressing and toileting and eating and physical help in bathing. (All four must
         be present and, together, they constitute one deficit.)
F2.      Requires extensive assistance (hands-on) with locomotion.
F3.      Requires extensive assistance (hands-on) to transfer.
F4.      Requires frequent (hands on) with bladder or bowel incontinent care, or with daily catheter or ostomy care.

A person can meet the intermediate level of care criteria in either of two ways: 1. by requiring at least one of the four numbered
intermediate services AND having one of the numbered functional deficits; OR 2. By having at least two of the numbered functional
deficits
