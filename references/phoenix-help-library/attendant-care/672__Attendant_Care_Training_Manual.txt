  Telephone Training Environment Operation -- EVV (Electronic Visit Verification)
  The South Carolina Care Call Training Environment provides an EVV environment with standard training
  scenarios to allow workers to practice use of the EVV in situations they are likely to encounter in normal
  operation. The training environment has been expanded to include scenarios for six Provider types, plus
  Nurse Supervisors, and EVV scenarios have been provided for both English and Spanish. However, the same
  training scenarios are shared by all Providers, so it is possible occasionally on checkout that a worker will
  hear their own check in, plus check in(s) from other worker(s) using the system. To make this less likely to
  become an issue, the environment includes two features:

    • Multiple sets of common scenarios – There are multiple copies of frequently used scenarios – e.g. 10
      copies of the In-Home Agency services scenarios. This means that if a collision with another trainer
      occurs on checkout, the trainer can simply pick a different worker id to use a different set of scenarios.

    • 30 Minute training checkout window – In the training environment, the checkout process only looks
      back 30 minutes to find matching check ins. This means that if a trainer checks in but does not check
      out, the orphan check in is ignored for training purposes after 30 minutes.

Both features make it less likely that trainers will conflict with each other and make it easy to adjust if they
do.

  Training Scenarios
  The Training Scenarios in the Training Environment are set up by provider type to mimic how workers for
  specific providers will use the Care Call system. Each scenario is selected by logging into the EVV with a
  corresponding worker id attached to the scenario. Scenarios have been set up as follows:

    • For EVV, the trainee dials the EVV phone number and logs in with the worker id for a training scenario.

    • The first four digits of the worker id select a Training provider record – For HIPAA compliance, these
      Training providers are all “made up” and not real providers. For common scenarios, like in-home
      agency care, the system provides up to ten providers, each with their own complete set of training
      scenarios. Since these are all equivalent, users can pick any one of the providers for each type and get
      the same training.

    • The last four digits of the worker id select a Training scenario for that provider -- For example, logging
      in with worker id 00101111 selects the first scenario in the table below for in-home agency training
      provider EN0010. In this example, seven scenarios are provided for each of ten in-home agency
      training providers (EN0010 – EN0019).

    • For some scenarios the worker must enter a CLTC number to identify a client – For these scenarios, the
      client CLTC numbers and names for the scenarios are given in the table on the next page.

INTRODUCTION
THE PURPOSE OF THE CLTC CARE CALL INDIVIDUAL ATTENDANT/COMPANION MANUAL IS TO Instruct
individual attendants and companions how to use the CARE CALL system.

The purpose of the CLTC Care Call Individual Attendant/ Companion Manual is to instruct individual
attendants and companions on how to use the Care Call system.

This easy-to-use system is for South Carolina Medicaid CLTC attendants and companions to use for logging
their participant visits. Care Call is a toll-free phone number that attendants/companions dial when
providing care to CLTC participants. The toll-free number is available anytime day or night, and allows the
attendant/companion to:

    •   Check In and Check Out when providing care at a participant’s home

    •   Enter their Worker ID, identify the Participant and identify Services during Check In/Out

 IT IS FAST!
 Your check in and check out will take about two minutes to complete.

 IT IS EASY TO USE!
 You will need access to the participant’s touch-tone landline phone, or you will use the EVV mobile app on
 your cell phoneforcheckingin/out. You will need to know your Worker ID number when using the EVV. If you
 have any problems, you should call your local CLTC area office representative as soon as possible.

 1. What is the EVV phone number?
    1-888-978-2273

 2. How does it work?
    As soon as you arrive at the participant’s home, you will call to check- in. You will
    provide the care to the participant and then call to check out.

 3. What information do I need to make the call to checkin?
        • The toll-free phonenumber
        • Your 8-digit worker ID number
        • The services being provided
        • The participant’s CLTC number

 4. What should I do if I do not hear my name when I call in?
    If you do not hear your name, hang up and try again. If you do not hear your name on the
    second call and you’re sure you put in the right number, still provide the care, and then
    submit a resolution log to the CLTC office.

 5. What if I’m providing two services (i.e., Attendant and Companion) to aparticipant?
    You check in for one service (i.e. Attendant), complete that service and check out. Then,
    check-in for the other service (i.e. Companion), complete the service and check-out.
    For example, if checking in for Attendant Care at 1:00 pm and checking out at 3:00 pm.
    Then, check in at 3:05 pm for Companion and check out at 4:05 pm.

 6. What if I’m serving two participants in the same home or two participants living in the
    same apartment complex or nearby?
    You must check in and out for both participants on separate calls.

 7. What should I do if my participant refuses to let me use the land-line telephone?
    Explain why you need to use the land-line telephone and that it is a toll-free call so they
    will not be charged. If the participant continues to refuse, provide care then notify the
    EOR and Case Manager the same day and submit a resolution log to the CLTC Office for
    corrections. You must use the Mobile App to check in/out for future visits, if you want to
    continue to serve this participant. A cell phone must be obtained, and you must start
    using the Mobile App forty-five (45) days from submission of the first resolution for this
    participant.

 8. What happens if I get to the participant’s home and the land-line phone is turned off?
    Provide the care, notify the EOR and submit a resolution log to the CLTC Office for
    corrections. You will report this issue to the Case Manager and use the Mobile App if
    available. If the land-line phone is not in operation within forty-five (45) days, you must
    start using the mobile app.

 9. What if I was able to check in but when I get ready to check out thephone has been
    turned off?
    Notify the EOR and Case Manager by the end of the workday and submit a resolution log
    to the CLTC Office for corrections. In order for services to continue to be authorized
    when no available phone can be identified or approved, the attendant must use the
    mobile app.

 10. What happens if I forget to checkin?
     Once you have completed providing services, you can check out. Then submit a
     resolution log to the CLTC office for corrections.

 11. What happens if I forgot to check out?
     Submit a resolution log to the CLTC office for corrections.

 12. What happens if I forget to check in and check out?
     Submit a resolution log to the CLTC office for corrections.




 13. What happens if I make a mistake while checking in or checking out?
     Hang up and call again. Your call will not be recorded until you hear “Your claim has
     been successfully recorded”.

 14. What happens if I’m still not sure my check in or check out phone call went through
     properly?
     Submit a resolution log to the CLTC office for verification and/or correction.

 15. If my participant goes to the hospital, can I go to the hospital and provide services?
     NO. Service can only be provided in the participant’s home, or another HOME approved
     and specified by the case manager. Service cannot be provided in places such as the
     hospital, nursing home, adult day care facility or rehabilitation facility.

 16. What happens if I go to my participant’s home and she’s not there? Can I still check into
     EVV?
     Since the participant is not home, you CANNOT check in or out of EVV. Notify the
     EOR.

 17. If I cannot provide services to my participant, can I share my worker ID with

Billing Procedures
Billing is done automatically for all visits made by CLTC providers to participants, as long as the visit
is provided as authorized. Checks will be issued by Morning Sun.

    Billing Procedures:
         ➢ Sunday through Saturday is considered a work week.
         ➢ Morning Sun issues checks with Friday’s date. Payment is twice amonth.
        ➢ All funds are direct deposited or placed on a money card. New workers will receive a
            paper check until this service is set up.

Did I get paid?
To be sure you got paid properly, follow these directions.
(Remember, this is only a suggestion, however, it has helped other attendants to follow these
directions).
     1. Use a calendar to record the dates, times, and services (Attendant or
         Companion) that you worked with your participant.
     2. When Morning Sun sends you the paycheck or a pay stub, listed on either the
         paycheck or pay stub will be the dates, hours, and services that you have been paid
         for. Compare these dates with the dates you worked.
     3. If you were paid for the date, then cross it off your calendar.
     4. If you were not paid for a date, you worked and you checked in/out of EVV
         properly, please contact the CLTC Area Office for assistance.
     5. Remember—the pay period is twice a month and runs from Sunday to Saturday.
         Please note that a State Holiday may impact the payment schedule.

                               Twelve Strike Policy
                    Personal Care I and II, Children’s Personal Care,
                     Attendant, Companion and Nursing Services
One strike will be given to a worker for each incident where a resolution must be done, and no
valid reason is given. After the twelfth strike, resolutions will no longer be accepted if no valid
and verified reason is given.

   •   The Electronic Visit Verification System includes Care Call and the mobile app. Workers
       who are new to the Electronic Visit Verification system (EVVS) will have a one-month
       grace period to learn the system before strikes begin to be issued. However, workers
       rehired or returning to work who will be using Care Call will not get this grace period
       since they should already know how to use CareCall.

   •   Workers will be given one strike per incident. For example, if worker is providing
       Personal Care I & II services for a participant and checks in for both services but fails to
       check out, only one strike will be given to correct both the PCI & PCII claims. If the
       worker checks in for multiple participants on the same call, only one strike will be given.

   •   After one year, each worker begins a new strike period. This means that the twelve-
       strike policy is an annual policy rather than a lifetime policy. For new workers, the
       annual date begins on the worker start date keyed in the worker registration section of
       Phoenix. The worker start date should always be the first day the worker will serve a
       participant to receive the full 30-day graceperiod.

   •   Each resolution not submitted timely will receive a strike. Providers must submit the
       resolution within 10 days from the Sunday following the Date of Service to be timely.
       Any resolutions submitted after that date will have strikes issued. These strikes will be
       assigned to the worker on the claim(s) although it is the responsibility of the provider
       agency to submit the resolutions timely.

   •   The Division of Long Term Living’s CLTC Area Office will have 14 days to respond to the
       provider agency resolutions that were submitted timely.

   •   Providers must use the participant’s landline phone or the mobile app to document
       services in the Electronic Visit Verification System. Any problems using the
       participant’s landline phone, or the mobile app should be reported immediately (by
       the next business day). The best way to notify the case manager is by sending a
       conversation in Phoenix. Attendants must notify the Case Managers by phone. The
       Employer of Record will be able to provide the Case Managers contact information. If
       the landline phone is disconnected or not working when the provider arrives, a
       resolution may be done. The provider must be prepared to have a mobile app available
       to serve the participant by the third visit when the landline phone is out of service. If
       the provider cannot/ will not begin using the mobile app, the provider must notify the
                                                                                                      29
         case manager by the third visit that they will no longer be able to provide services to
         the participant. The provider may continue to serve the participant, at the case
         manager’s request, until a new provider can be set up. No strikes will be given if this
         arrangement is made and documented.

 •       Providers may run the Infractions by Worker report on the Phoenix Provider Portal
         website to monitor the worker strikes.

Examples of strikes

     •    Attendant forgets to check in but does check out
     •    Attendant forgets to check out but does check in
     •    Attendant forgets to check in and worker forgets to check out
     •    Resolutions submitted late
