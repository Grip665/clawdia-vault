 Phoenix is a web-based software system.
 Phoenix is CLTC’s comprehensive participant tracking
  system.
 Referrals, authorizations and terminations are created and
  transmitted through Phoenix.
 Phoenix is used for service monitoring and reporting.
 Phoenix submits billing for CLTC services.
 The preferred web browser for Phoenix is Google Chrome.
 Chrome is a free web browser that may be downloaded at
  www.google.com/chrome
 When a service is needed, participants choose
  providers from a randomly generated list of all
  providers in their area.
 Participants order their choice of providers.
 Case managers make referrals in the order
  chosen by the participant.
 Only information contained in the referral will be shared
  with providers.
 Providers are prohibited from asking for additional
  information.
 Case managers and nurses are prohibited from sharing
  additional information during the referral process.
 Information shared with the provider is based on the service
  (e.g. A personal care provider is given information on the
  home condition, but an incontinence supplies provider is
  not).
 Phoenix sends providers an e-mail stating they have a
  referral (provider’s e-mail address must be up to date in
  Phoenix).
 Phoenix will direct the provider to the Provider Portal to view
  the referral.
 Referrals must be acted upon within two working days (48
  hours) excluding weekends and state holidays.
 If the referral is declined or not acted upon within two
  working days, Phoenix automatically sends the referral to
  the next provider.
 If the referral is accepted by the provider, the case
  manager is notified and will enter an authorization
  in Phoenix.
 Once the authorization is completed by the case
  manager, the provider receives an e-mail directing
  them to Phoenix to access and accept the
  authorization.
 When a service is terminated, providers also
  receive an e-mail directing them to Phoenix to view
  the termination.

 The provider will only get enough information to make a
  decision to accept or decline the request.

  service.
 The provider may accept or decline the referral the with or
  without comments.
 If a referral is accepted, it moves from the Notifications tab
  to the Pending Tab.

The missed visit reasons are as follows:
   o Attended less than 5 hours – this would be used by ADHC agencies
     who can’t bill if the participant did not stay at least 5 hours.
   o Holiday – the agency was closed due to a holiday.
   o Missed Visit – the participant desired services and the agency was
     not able to provide them as authorized.
   o Service Interruption – the participant is unavailable for services. For
     example, the participant was in the hospital or the participant
     declined services that day.
   o Other – a reason that does not fit into any of the above categories.
     This should be rarely used and the case manager should be notified
     why the service was not provided.

 Care Call is an Interactive Voice Response System (IVRS)
  used to document in-home services.
 Workers call a toll free phone number, 1-888-978-
  CARE(2273), using the participant’s landline phone to
  document service delivery.
 Providers of Children’s Medical Daycare use the facility’s
  phone to check in/out.
 Care Call recognizes the CLTC participant’s authorized
  phone number(s), the worker ID and the provider.
 Workers have the ability to select which service(s) will be
  performed.
 Services such as Personal Care I/II, Attendant, Children’s
  Medical Daycare and Case Management require two phone
  calls, a check-in call and a check-out call.
 Billing is submitted for the amount of time between the
  check-in call and the check-out call.
 Services such as Pest Control and Nurse Supervisory Visit
  require one call into Care Call.
 Workers should always have the participant’s seven digit
  CLTC number available in case Care Call does not recognize
  the participant’s authorized phone number and requests
  that the number be keyed.
 Workers should always listen to make sure they hear the
  correct worker name, provider name, participant name and
  service called back.
 If something is incorrect, the worker should correct it or
  hang up and start the call again.
 Workers of personal care, nursing or case management
  services are required to enter the activities (tasks) done
  during the visit in order to check-out.
 Care Call users may enter observations if they have any new
  observations which have not been reported to the case
  manager previously.
 Workers may check in for multiple participants on the same
  phone call(except case management).
 Workers may check in for multiple services on the same
  phone call if they are like services such as PCI, PCII and/or
  Companion Agency.
 When working a split schedule (perform a service twice a
  day) some adjustments may need to be made on the phone
  calls. For example, if a worker does PCI and PCII services in
  the morning and then comes back to do PCII again in the
  evening, the worker must check in/out for each service
  individually to be paid correctly.

 The Six Strike policy applies to providers of Personal Care I and II,
  Children’s Personal Care, Attendant, Companion and Nursing
  services.
 One strike will be given to a worker for each incident where a
  resolution must be done and no valid reason is given.
 After the sixth strike, CLTC staff will no longer accept resolutions
  for that worker if no valid and verified reason is given.
 Workers who are new to the Care Call system will have a one
  month grace period to learn the system. Re-hired workers will not
  get this grace period.
 After one year, each worker begins a new strike period.
 Workers will be given one strike per incident, per participant.
 Resolutions not submitted timely will receive a strike.
 Providers must submit the resolution within 10 days from
  the Sunday following the date of service to be timely.
 Providers must inform the case manager of any problems
  using the agreed upon phone or the mobile app by the next
  business day to avoid receiving strikes.
 We recommend using the conversation feature in Phoenix
  to report problems using the participant’s phone.
 Users may run the Infractions By Worker report in Phoenix
  to monitor worker strikes.
 Contact the area office for resolution problems or questions
