                                   Managed Care Organizations (MCOs) Fact Sheet

When a CLTC waiver applicant is enrolled in an incompatible MCO, the applicant must choose to remain in the MCO or
enter the waiver. The RSP overlap chart is used to determine if the MCO may overlap with CLTC. Phoenix will not allow
waiver enrollment if the applicant is a member of an incompatible MCO. In order to determine if an applicant is a
member of a MCO in Phoenix, click on the applicant’s “Dashboard”- “General Information”- “RSP”. Managed Care
Plans with an RSP of MCCM may overlap with CLTC waiver programs. Managed Care Plans with an RSP of MCHM may
not overlap with CLTC waivers. Managed Care Plans may overlap with the Children’s Personal Care Program.

Generally speaking the MCO’s benefit package is the same as Medicaid Fee for Service (State Plan Benefits). The biggest
difference is that MCO members do not have limits on the number physician office visits like Medicaid Fee for Service
(FFS). Some MCOs do not require a co-pay on a certain pharmaceuticals.

A comparison of MCO health plans may be found at the following web link. On the webpage, click the details button to
view specific plan information.

Once an applicant has decided to dis-enroll from a MCO to enter a CLTC waiver, the CLTC worker should visit
www.scdhhs.gov to complete the Waiver/PACE Enrollment form. This form is found under “Reference Tools”, “Waiver/
PACE enrollment.” Once the MCO termination is reflected in Phoenix, the applicant may be enrolled in a waiver.
Note: The applicant or primary contact should be encouraged to speak with their primary care provider and/or
pharmacist and use the resources provided above in order to make the best choice for his/her situation. This information
is related to Managed Care Organizations. Information about PRIME may be found in the PRIME FAQ document.
